Background: One Equation, Many Distances

The Friedmann Equation Contents decide history

For a uniform universe, Einstein’s equations reduce to one equation for the expansion rate H = ᵳ/a. Every ingredient contributes according to how fast it thins out as space grows:

H²(a) / H0²  =  Ωra−4 + Ωma−3 + Ωka−2 + ΩΛa−3(1+w)

Matter is diluted by volume (a−3). Radiation also loses energy to redshift (a−4). A cosmological constant (w = −1) does not dilute at all. The acceleration equation, ä/a = −½H0² Σ Ωi(1+3wi)a−3(1+wi), says that anything with w < −1/3 pushes instead of pulls. This page integrates that equation forwards and backwards from today, so bounces, turnarounds and crunches come out by themselves.

Redshift Is Stretching Not a Doppler shift

Light’s wavelength grows in step with space while it travels, so redshift measures how much the Universe has grown since the light left:

1 + z  =  a(t0) / a(temit)  =  1 / aemit

Light from z = 1 left when the Universe was half its present size. The CMB, at z ≈ 1090, left when it was about a thousandth.

Four Distances Each answers a different question

In an expanding universe “distance” needs a when and a how measured. With the comoving distance χ = c∫dt/a = c∫dz/H:

DM = Sk(χ),   DA = DM/(1+z),   DL = DM(1+z),   Dlt = c(t0 − temit)

Comoving: where the object is now, measured with rulers frozen at today’s size. Angular diameter: what you would infer from apparent size, because the light left when the object was much closer to us. Luminosity: what you would infer from brightness. Photons are both redshifted and spread out in time, so faint objects look extra far. Light travel: c times how long the light was in flight. Sk is sinh for an open universe and sin for a closed one, so in a closed universe DA can fall to zero at the antipode.

Horizons The edges of seeing

The Hubble sphere (radius c/H) is where recession speed equals c. It is not a horizon: light emitted just outside it is first carried away, but if the sphere grows it can catch the light. The particle horizon bounds everything whose light has had time to reach us since the Big Bang. That is the observable universe. The event horizon, which exists only if the expansion accelerates forever (or ends in a crunch), bounds the events whose light will ever reach us. Galaxies keep fixed comoving coordinates. In proper coordinates they fan outward. The past light cone is a teardrop: early light was emitted close to us and then carried away by the expansion before it could make progress.

What this page leaves out

The Universe here is perfectly smooth. There are no galaxies forming, no peculiar velocities, no gravitational lensing. Neutrinos are treated as massless radiation (Ωrh² = 4.18×10−5, photons plus Neff = 3.046), dark energy has a constant w, and inflation is not modelled, so the “Big Bang” is simply where a → 0. The history is computed with a 4th-order Runge–Kutta integration of the acceleration equation in steps of Δln a ≈ 0.0025, matched to analytic power laws at the singular ends. Results agree with standard calculators (e.g. astropy) to better than 0.1% for ΛCDM.
